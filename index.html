ANIMALS

KOALA TALKS

Koalas are iconic, tree-dwelling marsupials from Australia, known for their fluffy ears, large nose, and grey fur, despite often being called "koala bears".

These solitary animals primarily eat eucalyptus leaves, a low-energy diet that requires them to sleep up to 20 hours a day.

They possess strong claws for climbing and a pouch for their young, called joeys, which stay with the mother for months after birth, making them vulnerable to habitat loss, disease, and bushfires.














KANGAROO JACK

Kangaroos are iconic marsupials from Australia, known for their powerful hind legs, large feet, and long tails used for balance while hopping at high speeds, but they cannot hop backward.

These herbivores live in social groups called mobs and carry their young, called joeys, in a pouch on the mother's abdomen, where the joey develops for months after birth.

With fur ranging from red to grey, they use their small front legs for grasping and boxing, and they are a symbol of Australia, representing forward movement.











PANDA CANE

The giant panda is a beloved, black-and-white bear native to the bamboo forests of central China.

It is known for its bulky body, round head, and distinctive eye patches, using a unique wrist bone as a "thumb" to grasp bamboo, which makes up nearly its entire diet, requiring immense daily consumption.

Though these solitary animals are facing threats from habitat loss and have slow reproduction rates.This makes conservation efforts crucial for their survival.
